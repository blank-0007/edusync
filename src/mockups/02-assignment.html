Resting membrane potential and the pump

The Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase moves three Na⁺ ions out of the cell for every two K⁺ ions it brings in, so each catalytic cycle exports one net positive charge. That electrogenic current, together with the K⁺ leak conductance, sets the resting potential near −70 mV. With the concentrations from Table 2 (Na⁺ 145 mM out / 12 mM in; K⁺ 4 mM out / 140 mM in), the pump holds the gradients that the leak channels then read out as voltage.

  • Stoichiometry: 3 Na⁺ out : 2 K⁺ in → −1 charge per cycle, i.e. a small outward current.
  • Steady state: pump current balances the inward Na⁺ leak and outward K⁺ leak, so Vm is stable rather than drifting.
  • Energy: one ATP per cycle; ouabain blocks the extracellular K⁺ site and stops the pump without touching the channels.

If ouabain is applied at 37 °C, the gradients run down over several minutes: intracellular Na⁺ rises, K⁺ falls, and the membrane depolarises gradually rather than failing suddenly. The effect is slower than blocking a channel directly, because the leak conductances keep the membrane near their Nernst potentials until the gradients themselves decayediting · autosaved 14:02

In summary, the pump does two jobs at once: it maintains the ion gradients and it contributes a net outward current. Ouabain removes the current first and the gradients second, which is why the depolarisation is gradual and why the cell can recover if the inhibitor is washed out.
