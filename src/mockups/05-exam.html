Explain why the Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase is described as electrogenic, and state one consequence of that property for the resting membrane potential of a neuron.
Use the concentrations in Table 2. Aim for 3–4 sentences; partial credit is available for correct stoichiometry.

The pump is electrogenic because each cycle moves three Na⁺ ions out of the cell and only two K⁺ ions in, so the cell loses one net positive charge per cycle. That outward current is separate from the ion gradients themselves, and it persists as long as ATP is available.

One consequence is that the resting membrane potential sits slightly more negative than the K⁺ equilibrium potential alone would predict, because the pump keeps contributing an outward current at steady state. If the pump is inhibited, the membrane depolarises as the gradients run down.
